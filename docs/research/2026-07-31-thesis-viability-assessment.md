## 1. Landscape summary

**Language-conditioned RL without generative models.** This was an active, well-populated field roughly 2017–2022: Hermann et al., *Grounded Language Learning in a Simulated 3D World* (arXiv 2017); Chevalier-Boisvert et al., *BabyAI* (ICLR 2019); Cideron et al., *HIGhER* — HER with language-goal relabeling (arXiv 2019, verified); Chan et al., *ACTRCE* — HER with natural-language goal representations (arXiv 2019); Colas et al., *IMAGINE* (NeurIPS 2020); Lynch & Sermanet, *Language Conditioned Imitation Learning over Unstructured Data* (LangLfP, RSS 2021); Nair et al., *LOReL* (CoRL 2021, verified as arXiv 2109.01115); Lynch et al., *Interactive Language: Talking to Robots in Real Time* (arXiv 2022) — the last of these already did **real-time mid-episode language redirection** at 87k-instruction scale with a frozen-ish encoder. Luketina et al.'s survey (IJCAI 2019) frames the whole pre-LLM program. The uncomfortable fact: this repo's Phase 1 result (frozen encoder → projection → goal → policy, with mid-episode redirection) is a small-scale instance of what Interactive Language demonstrated in 2022, and the goal-relabeling machinery is HIGhER/ACTRCE territory from 2019.

**Interactive correction / repair.** Dense and recent: Co-Reyes et al., *Guiding Policies with Language via Meta-Learning* (ICLR 2019) — iterative language corrections as a learning signal, meta-learned; Sharma et al., *Correcting Robot Plans with Natural Language Feedback* (arXiv/RSS 2022) — corrections → cost-map transformations; Cui et al., *"No, to the Right" / LILAC* (HRI 2023) — online corrections warping a shared-autonomy control space; Karamcheti et al., *LILA* (CoRL 2021); Liu et al., *OLAF* (arXiv 2023) — verbal correction → policy update; Zha et al., *DROC* (arXiv 2023) — distilling corrections into retrievable knowledge; Shi et al., *Yell At Your Robot* (arXiv 2024); Bucker et al., *LaTTe* (arXiv 2022) — language-driven trajectory reshaping with frozen BERT/CLIP. Also Appelgren & Lascarides, *Learning Manner of Execution from Partial Corrections* (arXiv 2023) — "no, gently" as a grounding signal. Repair-as-signal is emphatically not open water.

**Style/manner conditioning.** Spiegel & Konidaris, *Guided Policy Search for Parameterized Skills using Adverbs* (arXiv 2021); Ruis et al., *gSCAN* (NeurIPS 2020) — compositional generalization with adverbs like "cautiously" as a benchmark axis; Juravsky et al., *PADL* (SIGGRAPH Asia 2022/2023) and Tessler et al., *CALM* (SIGGRAPH 2023) — language/latent-directed physics-based character control, exactly the "game NPC told to act differently" use case; MotionCLIP (Tevet et al., ECCV 2022) for text→motion style; Lotfi et al. (arXiv 2024) — adverbs → speed settings for navigation; Hristov & Ramamoorthy (ICLR 2021) — weakly supervised disentanglement of manner concepts from demonstrations; TacStyle (arXiv 2026) — language-selected execution style from structured latents.

**Frozen-embedding geometry and limits.** Conneau et al., *What you can cram into a single vector* (ACL 2018); Ethayarajh, *How Contextual are Contextualized Word Representations?* (EMNLP 2019) — anisotropy; documented negation blindness (SNCSE, arXiv 2022; Jina Embeddings, arXiv 2023, which built a negation dataset precisely because models fail it); Opitz & Frank, *S³BERT* (AACL 2022) — decomposing sentence embeddings into interpretable semantic features. There is **no robotics-facing taxonomy of frozen-embedding grounding failure modes with diagnostic signatures** found — that specific gap appears real. But the NLP-side probing program that would be its foundation peaked ~2018–2020.

**The SOTA positioned against.** SayCan (2022), Code-as-Policies (2022), Voyager (2023), RT-2 (2023), PaLM-E (2023), OpenVLA (Kim et al., arXiv 2024, verified). Notably, the small-encoder position is *not* dead in deployment: SVLR (arXiv 2025) uses all-MiniLM for retraining-free robot control on consumer GPUs, and Bong et al. (arXiv 2026) show a MiniLM/mpnet ensemble beating 120B-param LLMs at fleet task-to-skill routing. Small frozen encoders are current engineering practice; they are just not a current *scientific frontier*.

## 2. Per-arc novelty verdicts

**Arc 1 — Manner/style conditioning. Verdict: blog-post-grade on FetchReach; workshop-grade elsewhere; not thesis-grade.**
(a) Closest work: PADL and CALM do language/latent-directed behavioral style for physics characters — the NPC motivating example is literally their demo. Spiegel 2021 and Appelgren 2023 ground adverbs into skill parameters. gSCAN made adverb-composition a benchmark in 2020. (b) Genuinely new: deriving the style axis as a *direction in a frozen sentence-embedding space* and showing continuous behavioral morphing along it. (c) That delta is an implementation choice, not a finding. Worse, on FetchReach "style" collapses to speed and path curvature of a 3D point-reach — there is no observer, no noise, no terrain, nothing for "stealthy" to mean. A reviewer will ask "stealthy with respect to what?" and there is no answer in this environment.

**Arc 2 — Repair learning. Verdict: workshop-to-borderline-thesis-grade — the strongest arc, but only after a testbed change and a reframing.**
(a) Closest work: LILAC (Cui et al., HRI 2023) is uncomfortably close — online language corrections that adapt a control mapping within-session, with a frozen language model, no LLM in the loop. Co-Reyes 2019 made corrections a learning signal; DROC persists corrections; GSA-VLN (arXiv 2025) explicitly studies adapting to an individual instructor's language style over time. (b) Genuinely new: *online local warping of the language→goal grounding map itself* (not the policy, not the control space) per-user within a session, plus the structured-transparency dialogue (exposing nearest reference sentence / inferred region / confidence) as the agent's side — no paper found doing exactly that combination, and the transparency mechanism is a legitimately distinct interaction design. (c) The catch: on FetchReach the goal space is ℝ³, so "more to the left" is *isomorphic to a vector offset* — repair degenerates into online 3D regression, which any reviewer will dismiss in one line. Repair is only interesting when the correction is **not** a translation in goal space (manner corrections, referent corrections, intent corrections). Per-user grounding adaptation with a frozen encoder is a real, under-occupied niche — but it needs a goal/behavior space rich enough that the warp is nontrivial.

**Arc 3 — Limits map / taxonomy. Verdict: workshop-paper-grade as a standalone; thesis-grade only as the organizing spine of a larger claim.**
(a) Closest work: Conneau 2018 / Ethayarajh 2019 probing; documented negation and surface-similarity failures (SNCSE, Jina, Blagec et al.); gSCAN's failure catalog for compositional grounding. (b) Genuinely new: a *control-interface-facing* taxonomy — failure modes of frozen embeddings specifically when used as the substrate for grounded command interfaces, each with a reproducible diagnostic signature (the vocabulary-convention collision is a genuinely nice specimen: 0% on one class across all configs while training loss → 0 is a crisp, falsifiable signature of a data-design failure). No one has published that taxonomy. (c) But the honest ceiling: this is "characterizing MiniLM's limits," and the NLP community will read it as five years late; the robotics community will read it as an engineering guideline. As a *chapter* that grounds a bigger claim, it's excellent. As *the* contribution, it's a strong workshop paper (e.g., an HRI or CoRL workshop) — not a thesis.

## 3. Constraint audit: is "no generative model" principled or a gimmick?

**Steelman FOR:** (1) Determinism and verifiability — a frozen, revision-pinned encoder gives reproducible, certifiable behavior; no LLM system can currently offer that, and safety-adjacent robotics genuinely cares. (2) Cost/latency/on-device — 22M params vs 7B+; SVLR and Bong et al. show this is live engineering practice in 2025–26, and the fleet-routing result (MiniLM ensemble beating 120B LLMs at a *narrow* task) proves the regime exists. (3) Scientific hygiene — with an LLM in the loop, you cannot attribute grounding capability to anything; a frozen encoder makes the capability boundary *measurable*. That last point is the only one that turns the constraint into a research position rather than a deployment preference.

**Steelman AGAINST:** The field didn't just move on aesthetically — it moved on because frozen sentence encoders demonstrably lack compositionality, negation, and instruction-sensitivity, and those limits were *already documented* by 2020–2022 in NLP. "How much conversational control fits in a frozen embedding space" risks the answer being "roughly what SentEval probing already implied, projected into 3D." The constraint is principled **only if the thesis's central output is the quantified frontier itself** — capability-per-parameter, with the repair mechanism as the knob that moves the frontier. If the constraint is just the backdrop while redoing 2019–2022 experiments minus the LLM, it's a gimmick.

Call: principled *as a measurement program*, gimmick *as an identity*. The original three-arc framing leaned identity.

## 4. Testbed audit

FetchReach-v4 fatally undersells all three arcs. It has: a 3-DoF point goal, no obstacles, no observers, no objects, no ambiguity of referent. Consequences: style has almost nothing to modulate (Arc 1); corrections are goal-space translations, making repair degenerate (Arc 2); and the limits taxonomy reduces to "which sentences map to which octant of a cube" (Arc 3). The existing engineering (pinning, CI, multi-seed protocol, adversarial review) transfers; the environment does not. Minimum viable upgrade: (a) FetchPush/PickAndPlace for referent-bearing goals, and (b) a navigation/locomotion env where manner is expressible — a gridworld/MiniGrid variant with observers and noise costs (stealth becomes *measurable*: detection events, noise budget), or gSCAN-style compositional grounding for the limits work. Real humans vs scripted repair: a thesis can survive on scripted repair distributions + a small (n≈10–15) human study for the transparency interface; a pure-scripted thesis on repair will draw the "you simulated the human, so you assumed the answer" objection — budget for one small study.

## 5. What the strongest version looks like

**Research question:** *What is the capability ceiling of a frozen small-encoder language interface for embodied control, where exactly does it break (diagnosable how), and how much of the gap can online conversational repair recover — all without a generative model?*

One thesis, three chapters, in this order:

1. **The ceiling (Arc 3, promoted to spine).** A taxonomy of frozen-embedding grounding failures as a *control interface*: vocabulary-convention collision (exists), negation blindness, manner–content entanglement, compositional novelty — each with a diagnostic signature (training-dynamics fingerprint like the 0%-class/loss→0 pattern) and a reproducible probe suite. Deliverable: the probe suite as an artifact others can run against any encoder. This generalizes beyond MiniLM — run it on 3–4 encoders (MiniLM, mpnet, GTE-small, a CLIP text tower) so it's a *method*, not a MiniLM autopsy.
2. **Repair recovers the gap (Arc 2, reframed).** Show that failures identified in Chapter 1 are *locally repairable*: within-session corrections warp the grounding map (e.g., residual local warps over the frozen space — cite and differentiate from LILAC and DROC explicitly) and quantifiably restore capability past the frozen ceiling, per-user, with the structured-transparency dialogue as the mechanism that makes repair sample-efficient (the agent exposes *why* it misunderstood, the human corrects the right thing). This is the headline: **repair as the cheap substitute for a generative model.** Small human study here.
3. **Manner as the hard case (Arc 1, demoted to a stress test).** Don't build a style-conditioning system; use manner words as the taxonomy's hardest category and repair's hardest target, in the navigation env where "carefully" is measurable. One chapter section, not an arc.

**Honest contribution claim:** "A diagnostic method for the grounding limits of frozen sentence encoders as control interfaces, and evidence that structured conversational repair recovers a quantified fraction of the capability gap at 1/300th the parameters of an LLM interface." That is defensible, novel in combination, and the no-LLM constraint becomes load-bearing rather than decorative.

## 6. Recommendation

**PIVOT** — keep the constraint, the reproducibility infrastructure, and the vocabulary-collision finding; kill the three-arc framing and FetchReach as the primary testbed.

Justification: Arcs 1 and 2 as originally framed have named prior work sitting almost on top of them (PADL/CALM for style; LILAC/Co-Reyes/DROC for repair), and FetchReach makes both degenerate — the strongest existing result in the repo (Phase 1) is itself a small-scale rediscovery of Lynch et al. 2022. The one asset with no direct competitor is the *diagnostic-signature* idea born from the repo's own negative result — it's falsifiable, and nobody has systematized it for grounded control. Build the thesis around measuring the ceiling and repairing past it; the existing infrastructure (pinning, multi-seed, adversarial review, the intent classifier and its failure) all carries forward, so this pivot wastes little.

**Biggest risk if proceeding unpivoted:** a long stretch producing a system whose every component has a 2021–2023 citation that a reviewer names in the first paragraph — "LILAC did the repair, PADL did the style, Interactive Language did the redirection; the delta is a smaller encoder and a simpler environment" — a rejection that was fully visible from here.
