# Demo clips

Clips 1-3 (stage 3) generated by
`experiments/03_language_goal_projection/make_demos.py` from
`experiments/03_language_goal_projection/checkpoints/seed_0.zip` (SAC+HER
policy, literal 1.000 eval success rate, unchanged across all stage-3
attempts 1-4). Clips 4-8 (stages 1, 2, 4, 5, 6) generated by each stage's own
`make_demo.py`, added later to cover the stages that previously had no
visual proof at all — see their entries below for checkpoints/instructions
used. Every clip's success/fail label below is the real,
`info["is_success"]`-grounded outcome of the exact recorded episode
(`lang_goal_rl.episode_recording.record_episode` for clips 1-6,
`record_episode_with_goal_switch` for clips 7-8) — never assumed.

**2026-07-27 seed-selection fix.** A pixel-diff audit found that clips 1, 4,
and 5 showed almost no visible movement after the first couple of frames,
and that clips 1 and 5 were byte-identical (same SHA1) despite being labeled
as different stages. Root cause: every `make_demo(s).py` script tried eval
seeds in increasing order and stopped at the *first* one that succeeded.
FetchReach-v4 samples goals only a few centimeters from a fixed reset pose,
so the first success is very often an almost-imperceptible nudge — a real
success, just visually boring on a wide camera framing the whole
robot+table. Clips 1 and 5 additionally load the *exact same checkpoint*
(`03_language_goal_projection/checkpoints/seed_0.zip`) and had both started
their search at the same seed (1000), landing on the identical episode.

The fix, applied to clips 1, 3, 4, 5, and 6: `EpisodeRecording` now reports
`total_travel` — the sum of per-step Euclidean displacement of the gripper's
`achieved_goal` across the whole episode (not just start-to-end distance, so
a trajectory that moves out and back doesn't look falsely static). Each
script now searches a small, bounded range of eval seeds (10-15), records
*every* real success found, and keeps the one with the largest
`total_travel`. This never fabricates or cherry-picks a non-success — only
real successes are compared — and where no tried seed had meaningfully more
travel than the first success would have given, that's reported below
honestly rather than forced. Clips 1/2/4/5's own seed ranges were also made
mutually disjoint so no two scripts can land on the same episode again, even
by coincidence. Clips 7 and 8 were audited too (see their entries) and
judged, with pixel-diff evidence, not to need this fix — the goal-switch
moment itself already produces large, sustained real motion in both.

## 1. `01_baseline_success.gif`
**Literal-goal mode** — no override, the policy sees whatever
`GoalEmbeddingExtractor` normally computes from the env's real
`desired_goal`. Searched `BASELINE_EVAL_SEEDS` (6000-6014, 15 seeds,
deliberately disjoint from clip 5's `EVAL_SEEDS` since both load this same
checkpoint) — all 15 succeeded. First success was seed 6000
(`total_travel=0.1140`); selected seed 6009 instead
(`total_travel=0.2089`, 1.83x more gripper displacement over the episode).
**Result: success (15/15 seeds tried succeeded; most travel-heavy real
success selected).** Consistent with this checkpoint's measured 1.000
literal-goal eval success rate over 50 episodes. Pixel-diff audit: mean
frame-to-frame difference rose from 0.167 (old, seed-1000 clip) to 0.232
(new, seed-6009 clip).

## 2. `02_broken_language_failure.gif`

input for the instruction *"reach up high"*. Ground truth is a real xyz goal
sampled from the "reach up high" region; one episode recorded, no

## 3. `03_language_goal_result.gif`
**Attempt 3's fixed-centroid-regression projection**
(`artifacts/language_goal_projection_v3.pt`) overriding the policy's
desired-goal input for the same instruction, *"reach up high"* — but now
evaluated under **attempt 4's corrected ground-truth protocol**
(`train.compute_region_centroid`): a single fixed xyz centroid for the
region, reused for every attempt, instead of clip 2's per-attempt resampled
random point. Attempt 3's own eval had measured only a 0.120 success rate
for this exact instruction against the old (resampled-point) ground truth;
the stage-3 reviewer diagnosed that ground-truth protocol itself — not the
projection — as the actual defect, since the policy only ever saw one fixed
embedding the whole time. Attempt 4's fix realigned the ground truth with
what that embedding represents, and re-measured 1.000 across all 3 seeds x
50 episodes for this instruction (and every other one).
**Result: success on the 1st of 1 seed tried, seed 9000.** No searching was
needed — the first attempt succeeded, consistent with attempt 4's measured
1.000 aggregate language-goal success rate (42/42 seed x instruction
samples, 3 seeds x 14 instructions x 50 episodes each), matching the 1.000
stage-2 literal-goal baseline the proof gate requires. This is now a
reliable success, not a lucky draw — contrast with the pre-fix framing this
same clip carried under attempt 3 (a 1-in-7 draw, kept here in git history
for the record).

**2026-07-27 seed-search finding (not a fix — a real negative result):**
this clip's ground truth is `train.compute_region_centroid`'s *fixed* xyz
centroid, reused unchanged across every attempt (that's the whole point of
attempt 4's fix — see above), and the episode's starting robot pose in
FetchReach-v4 isn't randomized either. So once the desired goal is pinned to
that fixed centroid, the entire trajectory is a deterministic function of
the checkpoint and the override embedding, independent of which eval seed
is passed in. Trying 12 seeds from `LANGUAGE_EVAL_SEEDS` (9000-9011) to
search for a more travel-heavy real success confirmed this directly: all 12
produced the exact same `total_travel=0.1318` and the exact same episode.
The search is reported honestly as a non-improvement rather than forced —
seed 9000 (the original pick) is unchanged and still the right choice, since
no other candidate was actually different.

saved to reuse), but a fresh run of the identical protocol — since stage 2's
proof gate is about whether that protocol works, not about one arbitrary
seed's weights, this is a faithful stand-in. Recorded in literal-goal mode

2's proof gate tests. Searched `EVAL_SEEDS` (2000-2014, 15 seeds — a new

**This clip's SHA1 is now confirmed different from clip 1's** — the
byte-identical-clip bug is resolved.

## 6. `06_stage4_open_vocabulary_result.gif`

**Stage 4 — a live English sentence, never used to train anything, actually
steering the robot.** Generated by
`experiments/04_open_vocabulary/make_demo.py` using
`src/lang_goal_rl/live_goal_controller.py`'s `LiveGoalController` (k=1
nearest-neighbor lookup over the 84-sentence combined vocabulary — stage 4's
final, passing mechanism) to convert an instruction into a goal embedding,
then `03_language_goal_projection/checkpoints/seed_0.zip` (same checkpoint
every stage-3/4 eval uses) to act on it. Stage 4's own `report.md` says
outright that no existing chart captures this passing result — every chart
on file was made for an earlier, failed attempt. This is that missing proof.

Two held-out instructions were tried, in this order, and both real outcomes
are reported — not cherry-picked:

1. **"raise your arm as high as it will go"** (region "reach up high",
   `held_out_paraphrases.HELD_OUT_PARAPHRASES`) — the task brief's own
   suggested example. Reading `report.md`'s Attempt 4 per-instruction tables
   directly (rather than assuming them) shows this *specific* phrase is
   misclassified under k=1 lookup (nearest region comes back "reach down
   low") and scores a documented 0.000 RL success on all 3 of stage 4's
   checkpoints — a deterministic outcome (fixed goal-embedding + fixed
   policy), not a per-seed draw, so retrying seeds would not have changed it.
   **Recorded outcome: failure**, exactly as the report predicts.
2. **"extend upward toward the ceiling"** — a second held-out phrase from
   the same "reach up high" region, which the same report table documents as
   correctly classified under k=1 and a reliable 1.000 RL success on all 3
   checkpoints. **Recorded outcome: success.** This is the clip saved to
   `06_stage4_open_vocabulary_result.gif`.

**Result: success on the 2nd of 2 held-out instructions tried.** For each
instruction, `EVAL_SEEDS` (9000-9014, 15 seeds) was searched in full rather
than stopping at the first attempt. The 1st instruction ("raise your arm...")
failed on **all 15/15** tried seeds, every one with the identical
`total_travel=0.1396` — a deterministic failure, not a per-seed draw,
confirming the report's documented 0.000 RL success rate for real rather
than assuming it. The 2nd instruction ("extend upward...") succeeded on
**all 15/15** tried seeds, every one with the identical `total_travel=0.1318`
(seed 9000, the first success, kept — no other seed produced a different
episode, since this eval's ground truth is a fixed region centroid and the
episode's starting pose isn't randomized, so the trajectory is deterministic
given the checkpoint and instruction, same finding as clip 3's search). The
1st instruction's failure is genuine and expected, not a bug — both outcomes
were independently confirmed by actually running the pipeline for every
tried seed, not taken from the report table on faith. Consistent with
attempt 4's aggregate k=1 held-out result (mean 0.571, median 1.000, 24/42
nonzero samples — a binary, per-instruction pattern of 8/14 phrases always
succeeding and 6/14 always failing across all 3 checkpoints, not a smooth
quality gradient), and with the reviewer's PASS verdict noting the k=1
mechanism "never deviates from a known-good point" for phrases it classifies
correctly.

## 7. `07_stage5_midepisode_switch.gif`

**Stage 5 — the robot's target changes partway through the episode, with no
reset, and it still gets there.** Generated by
`experiments/05_midepisode_regoal/make_demo.py` using
`lang_goal_rl.episode_recording.record_episode_with_goal_switch` against
`experiments/01_uvfa_her_baseline/checkpoints/seed_0.zip` — the same healthy
seed clip 4 uses (seeds 2 and 7 excluded for the same documented SAC
deterministic-eval-collapse reason). Literal-xyz mode throughout (no
embedding override): the policy sees the env's real `desired_goal` in both
phases, matching stage 5's own scope of testing the re-goaling mechanism
itself, not the embedding layer stages 2-4 cover.

Goal pair: a point sampled from the "reach left" region, switched at step 20
of a 50-step episode to a point sampled from the "reach up high" region —
two regions picked for visual clarity (the two goals are unmistakably
different on screen), one of the four switch points (10/20/30/40)
`run_regoal_eval.py` actually measured. Success is judged against the *new*
("reach up high") goal only — any success against the old goal before the
switch never counts.

**Result: success on the 1st of 3 attempts tried (seed 10000 — the exact
`base_seed` `run_regoal_eval.py` itself uses for switch_step=20's first
episode).** This is the visual counterpart to stage 5's own measured result:
across 8/10 healthy seeds, the swap condition equalled the budget-matched
baseline equalled the full-budget reference — 1.000 == 1.000 == 1.000 — at
every switch_step tested (`experiments/05_midepisode_regoal/report.md`). The
10-seed statistical result already showed the re-goaling mechanism adds no
measurable extra difficulty; this clip is that same finding made visible —
one real episode where the robot is given a completely different mid-episode
target and still reaches it.

**2026-07-27 audit, not regenerated (judgment call):** unlike clips 1/4/5,
this clip was *not* re-selected by `total_travel` search. Pixel-diff shows
two large, sustained motion bursts -- mean abs frame difference peaks at 1.38
over steps 0-2 (initial reach) and again at 1.96 over steps 20-23 (the
switch itself), each decaying back to a near-static ~0.03-0.05 baseline
within about 6-8 frames as the arm reaches and holds its target (expected
behavior for a real success, not a bug). Overall mean frame difference is
0.28, well above clip 1/4/5's old (pre-fix) values of ~0.13-0.17. Both
regions ("reach left", "reach up high") were also deliberately hand-picked
for visual separation, which is why "first attempt" here (seed 10000) didn't
need a travel search the way the literal-mode clips did. Judged sufficiently
dynamic as-is; the switch moment is the compelling part of this clip.

## 8. `08_stage6_live_english_capstone.gif`

**Stage 6 — the capstone clip.** Type an English instruction, the robot
goes for it; type a different instruction partway through, it redirects
live — no reset, no retraining, nothing rehearsed. Every prior stage's
mechanism runs at once here: a live sentence becomes a goal embedding
(stage 4's `LiveGoalController`, k=1 nearest-neighbor lookup over the
84-sentence combined vocabulary), that embedding drives the same
embedding-based policy stages 2/3 trained, and the swap happens mid-episode
with no reset (stage 5's mechanism) — this is the single visual that makes
the whole project's thesis obvious without reading a number.

Generated by `experiments/06_live_english_interface/make_demo.py` using
`lang_goal_rl.episode_recording.record_episode_with_goal_switch`'s
embedding-override mode against
`experiments/03_language_goal_projection/checkpoints/seed_0.zip` (the same
checkpoint every stage 3-6 script reuses). Both instructions are drawn from
`set_b_vocabulary.SET_B_INSTRUCTIONS` — stage 6's 7 brand-new phrasings,
verified disjoint from every sentence this project has ever trained or
tested on — rather than one of Set A's already-measured paraphrases, since
Set B is this project's strongest "someone just typed something ad-hoc"
story.

Instruction pair: *"send the arm climbing toward the highest point it can
reach"* (region "reach up high"), switched at step 20 of a 50-step episode
to *"let the arm descend toward the lowest point it can reach"* (region
"reach down low") — the two most visually opposite regions in the
workspace (straight up vs. straight down), so the redirect is unmistakable
on screen. Success is judged against the *new* ("reach down low")
instruction's region centroid only, exactly as `live_regoal_eval.py`'s own
switch suite judges it.

**Result: success on the 1st of 3 attempts tried (seed 70000).** No
searching was needed. This is the same mechanism stage 6's own aggregate
run measured across 3 checkpoints and both vocabulary sets: mid-episode
live-language switches reached the new target about as often as being
given that instruction from the very start (Set A switch: 0.548 task
success, tracking Set A's 0.571 no-switch baseline; Set B switch: 0.857,
tracking Set B's own 0.857 no-switch baseline) and, when redirection
happened, it was fast — median 3 steps after the switch for both sets, out
of 30 steps of post-switch budget remaining
(`experiments/06_live_english_interface/report.md`). This clip is that same
finding made visible: one real episode, one live instruction swapped for
another, the robot visibly changing its mind and reaching the new target.

**2026-07-27 audit, not regenerated (judgment call):** same treatment as
clip 7. Pixel-diff shows an even more dynamic profile than clip 7: mean abs
frame difference peaks at 1.45 over steps 0-2 (initial reach) and stays high
-- 1.3-1.6 -- for a full 6 frames around the switch (steps 20-25) before
gradually decaying, indicating a longer, more visible redirect. Overall mean
frame difference is 0.42, the highest of any clip in this set (old or new).
Judged sufficiently dynamic as-is; not regenerated.

## 9. `09_stage10_typed_command_capstone.gif`

**Stage 10 (Phase 2a) — the typed-command capstone.** One continuous
50-step episode, no reset: a `goto` command drives the robot to an
absolute point, a `move` command shifts it relative to wherever it
actually ended up, and a `waypoints` command then chains it through a
short sequence of further legs — all three parsed from plain command
strings through the real `parse_command` → `CommandExecutor` → env/policy
pipeline (`experiments/10_typed_command_interface/make_demo.py`), the same
pipeline `interactive_demo.py --interface commands` drives live. Uses one
of the same 8 healthy checkpoints every Phase 2a stage validates against.

**This clip is illustrative, not a statistical claim** — the actual
proof-gate numbers (goto/move/waypoint success rates, malformed-input
rejection, stop-hold drift) come from a separate scripted harness across
all 8 healthy seeds; see
`experiments/10_typed_command_interface/report.md`.
